    Going around now

    Tricks scammers use on calls

    Tap a panel to see what they say and what to do. Each one is a scam.

    • Fake police, CBI or customs, often on a video call, demanding money.

      They will say: Do not hang up and do not tell anyone.

      Do this now: Hang up. No police officer, CBI, ED, customs officer or judge arrests anyone over a video call.

      Call 1930

    • A support agent who needs your OTP, PIN or card number.

      They will say: We are sending an OTP, please read it out to verify you.

      Do this now: Hang up. Banks never ask for an OTP, PIN or CVV. Call your bank on the number printed on your card.

      Call 1930

    • A familiar voice in sudden trouble, asking for money fast.

      They will say: I am in an accident, hospital or police trouble. Do not tell anyone, just send the money now.

      Do this now: Do not send money on a voice call alone. Call that person back on the number saved in your phone, and ask for your family code word.

      Call 1930

    • Asked to install an app or share your screen to fix something.

      They will say: Install this app so we can process your refund.

      Do this now: Stop sharing your screen and close the app. Switch on airplane mode and do not open your banking or UPI apps.

      Call 1930

    The script

    How a scam unfolds

    Most scams follow the same five steps. Spot the stage you are in and stop before the last one. Paste a whole chat above and ScamShield finds these stages for you.

    1. 1

      Hook

      A lure: prize, job, parcel, refund or an urgent family story.

    2. 2

      Fake authority

      Claims to be a bank, agency, utility or official.

    3. 3

      Pressure

      Threats and deadlines to stop you thinking.

    4. 4

      Isolation

      Tells you to keep it secret or stay on the line.

    5. 5

      Extraction

      Asks for money, OTP or PIN, an app install or a call-back.

    Keep your family safe

    Four rules to remember

    1. 1

      Police, CBI and customs never arrest anyone over a call or video. Hang up.

    2. 2

      Never share an OTP, PIN or CVV with anyone, ever.

    3. 3

      Never install an app or share your screen because a caller asked.

    4. 4

      If someone you know asks for money, call them back on their saved number first.

    One habit that stops voice scams

    Set a family code word

    Pick a word that is not on social media and cannot be guessed from your photos. Tell it to close family in person or on a call, and never write it in a chat. If anyone asks for money in an emergency, ask for the code word first. No code word means no money.

    Questions

    Quick answers

    Is ScamShield free?

    Yes. There is no sign-up, no app to install and no advertising.

    Does it send my message anywhere?

    No. The check runs inside your browser on your device. The page has a strict security policy that stops it from making network requests.

    Can it be wrong?

    Yes. It is a rule-based checker and new tricks can get past it. If a message asks for money, a code or an app, check with someone you trust first.

    I already paid. What should I do?

    Call 1930 as soon as you can and report the fraud at cybercrime.gov.in. Choose "I sent money" in the checker above for the steps in order.

    Who is it for?

    Anyone in India who gets suspicious calls or messages, and the families who look out for them.
