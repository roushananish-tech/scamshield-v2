## 1. "I lost money" plans

Disclaimer shown under every plan: Guidance only, not legal advice. Reporting early gives banks more to act on, but no step can guarantee that money is returned.

### I sent money by UPI or bank transfer  (`upi_paid`)

_Act in the next few minutes. The steps below are in the order that matters._

1. **Call 1930 now** (urgent) [helpline]  
   National cyber-fraud helpline, toll-free, open all day. Give the amount, time, bank or wallet and the transaction ID. The report is passed to the banks so that the specific amount can be put on hold. This does not replace calling your bank.
2. **Call your bank and block the route** (urgent) [bank]  
   Use the number printed on your card or inside the official bank app, never a number from a message. Ask them to block the card, UPI or net banking and to flag the transaction. Write down the complaint number and the time of the call.
3. **Report to the bank in writing within 3 working days** [bank]  
   Under RBI rules, the time you take to report an unauthorised electronic transaction decides your liability: zero if it was a third-party breach and you report within 3 working days, limited if you report in 4 to 7 working days. The bank must credit the amount temporarily within 10 working days while it investigates. If you yourself shared a code or approved the payment, a bank may treat the case differently, so do not assume a refund. Report today anyway.
4. **File a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in** [portal]  
   Choose the financial-fraud option, enter the same details and screenshots, and save the acknowledgement number it gives you.
5. **Keep every piece of evidence** [you]  
   Do not delete chats, call logs or messages. Screenshot the phone number, UPI ID, account number, links, app names and payment receipts.
6. **Do not pay "recovery agents"** [you]  
   Anyone offering to get your money back for a fee, or asking you to pay again to release your funds, is running a second scam.
7. **Report the number or message** [portal]  
   At sancharsaathi.gov.in use "Report Suspected Fraud Communication" (Chakshu).

## 2. "I am on a call right now" cards

### Someone says I am under "digital arrest", or claims to be police, CBI or customs  (`digital_arrest`)

**Hang up. This is a scam.**

Do now:
1. End the call or video chat. You are free to.
2. No police officer, CBI, ED, customs officer or judge arrests or questions anyone over a video call.
3. Tell someone near you now, or call a family member on their saved number.
4. Do not transfer money, not even to a "safe", "RBI" or "verification" account.

They will say:
- Do not hang up and do not tell anyone.
- Your Aadhaar, SIM or a parcel in your name is linked to a crime.
- Pay now to clear your name and the money will be returned.

Afterwards:
- Report at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.
- If you already paid, open the "I lost money" plan.

### A voice that sounds like someone I know is asking for money  (`voice_clone`)

**Do not send money on a voice call alone.**

Do now:
1. Hang up.
2. Call that person back on the number saved in your phone, or ask someone else who can reach them.
3. Ask for the family code word, or a question only the real person could answer.
4. Pressure to pay within minutes is the scam. Slow down.

They will say:
- I am in an accident, hospital or police trouble.
- Do not tell anyone, just send the money now.
- My phone is broken, this is a new number.

Afterwards:
- Agree a family code word, a secret word that only your family knows, and keep it offline.
- If you already paid, open the "I lost money" plan.

### Someone claiming to be my bank, card company or a support agent is on the line  (`bank_caller`)

**Hang up. Banks never ask for an OTP, PIN or CVV.**

Do now:
1. Hang up.
2. Call your bank on the number printed on your card or inside its official app.
3. Do not share any OTP, PIN, CVV or password, whatever they say.
4. Do not install any app or share your screen.

They will say:
- Your card or account is blocked or compromised.
- We are sending an OTP, please read it out to verify you.
- Install this app so we can fix it.

Afterwards:
- If you shared a code, call your bank at once and open the "I lost money" plan.
- Report the number at sancharsaathi.gov.in.

### Someone asked me to install an app or share my screen  (`remote_help`)

**Stop sharing your screen and close the app now.**

Do now:
1. End the call and close or uninstall the app (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, QuickSupport or similar).
2. Switch on airplane mode.
3. Do not open your banking or UPI apps.
4. Then open the "I lost money" plan and choose the remote-access option.

They will say:
- Install this app so we can process your refund.
- Read me the 9-digit code on your screen.
- Open your banking app so I can check it.

Afterwards:
- Change your banking and UPI passwords from a different device.
- Call your bank on the number on your card.

### Four rules

1. Police, CBI and customs never arrest anyone over a call or video. Hang up.
2. Never share an OTP, PIN or CVV with anyone, ever.
3. Never install an app or share your screen because a caller asked.
4. If someone you know asks for money, call them back on their saved number first.

### Set a family code word

1. Pick a word or phrase that is not on social media and cannot be guessed from your photos.
2. Tell it to your close family in person or on a call, and never write it in a chat.
3. If anyone asks for money in an emergency, ask for the code word first. No code word means no money.

## 3. "Spot the scam" cards

- **SCAM** (WhatsApp video call, then chat): "This is Officer Mehta from the Cyber Cell. A SIM bought with your Aadhaar is linked to money laundering. You are under digital arrest. Stay on this video call and do not tell anyone."
  - Lesson: Nobody is arrested over a video call, and "do not tell anyone" is how scammers cut you off from help.
- **SCAM** (WhatsApp): "Our previous group was banned by WhatsApp. Join the new VIP group, members get daily sure shots and big profits. Tap to join before seats are full."
  - Lesson: "The old group was banned, join the new one" and "VIP group" are on SEBI's list of stock-tip scam signs.
- **SCAM** (SMS): "Dear customer, your account will be blocked today. Update your KYC now: http://secure-kyc-update.top/verify"
  - Lesson: Banks do not do KYC through links. Look at the link: a random ".top" address, not your bank.
- **SCAM** (WhatsApp, unknown number): "Hi Mum, this is my new number, my phone fell in water. I need to pay a hospital bill urgently. Can you transfer Rs 25,000 to this account? I will explain later."
  - Lesson: A new number plus an urgent money request is the standard family scam. Call your child on the old number first.
- **SCAM** (Telegram): "Great job, 3 tasks done and commission Rs 450 added! To unlock the next task group you must recharge Rs 3,000 into your merchant account. It will be refunded with extra commission."
  - Lesson: Small payouts first, then a "deposit" to continue. That deposit never comes back.
- **SCAM** (Phone call, then SMS): "Your refund of Rs 4,999 is ready. Install AnyDesk and tell our agent the 9-digit code so we can send it to your account right now."
  - Lesson: A remote-access app lets a stranger see your screen and OTPs. Real refunds never need one.
- **LEGIT** (SMS from your bank): "Rs 1,200.00 debited from A/c XX1234 on 21-Nov via UPI to Metro Mart. If not you, call your bank's helpline."
  - Lesson: An alert about a payment you made, with no link and no request. Telling you to call your bank is the safe advice.
- **LEGIT** (SMS): "Your OTP for login is 394017. It is valid for 10 minutes. Do not share it with anyone."
  - Lesson: This sends a code and warns you not to share it. It becomes a scam only when someone asks you to read it out.
- **LEGIT** (SMS from a shop): "Hi, your order from the pharmacy will arrive today between 4 and 6 pm. You can track it in the app you ordered from."
  - Lesson: No link, no payment, no pressure. It points you to the app you already use.

## 5. Detection rules (title and explanation shown to users)

- `urgency` [urgency, weight 0.25] **Artificial urgency**: Scammers rush you so you act before thinking. Real organisations rarely demand action within minutes.
- `threat_loss` [threat, weight 0.35] **Threat of losing an account or service**: Fear of losing access is the most common pressure tactic.
- `threat_hinglish` [threat, weight 0.4] **Threat of closure or arrest (Hinglish)**: Same fear tactic written in romanised Hindi.
- `threat_devanagari` [threat, weight 0.4] **Threat of closure or arrest (Hindi)**: Fear tactic written in Hindi.
- `urgency_hinglish` [urgency, weight 0.25] **Artificial urgency (Hinglish/Hindi)**: Pressure to act immediately.
- `digital_arrest` [authority, weight 0.65] **"Digital arrest" style threat**: No agency arrests or interrogates anyone over a call or chat. This script is a known scam.
- `authority_agency` [authority, weight 0.2] **Claims to be from an agency or authority**: Scammers pose as police, courts or regulators to scare people into obeying.
- `isolation` [isolation, weight 0.55] **Tells you to keep it secret or stay on the line**: Isolating the victim from family and advice is a hallmark of serious scams.
- `kyc` [kyc, weight 0.35] **KYC / account-verification demand**: Banks do not ask you to verify KYC through links or messages.
- `utility` [utility, weight 0.5] **Utility disconnection threat**: Fake "electricity bill unpaid, power cut tonight" texts are a widespread scam.
- `telecom` [authority, weight 0.4] **SIM / mobile-number block threat**: Telecom operators do not threaten disconnection through random links.
- `traffic` [authority, weight 0.35] **Traffic fine / FASTag lure**: Fake e-challan and FASTag messages push victims to pay through a fraudulent site or app.
- `credential_ask` [credential, weight 0.6] **Asks for OTP, PIN, password or card details**: No bank, agency or company will ever ask you to share an OTP, PIN or CVV.
- `credential_hinglish` [credential, weight 0.6] **Asks for OTP or PIN (Hinglish/Hindi)**: Handing over an OTP or PIN lets the scammer drain your account.
- `credential_mention` [credential, weight 0.1] **Mentions an OTP or PIN**: Mention alone is normal in genuine OTP messages; it only matters with other signals.
- `payment_fee` [payment, weight 0.5] **Asks you to pay a small "fee" first**: Advance fees to release a parcel, prize, loan or refund are a classic scam.
- `payment_instruction` [payment, weight 0.3] **Instructs you to send money**: Unexpected requests to pay or transfer money deserve suspicion.
- `upi_collect` [payment, weight 0.35] **UPI collect / QR trick**: Receiving money never requires entering your UPI PIN or scanning a QR code.
- `payment_hinglish` [payment, weight 0.35] **Asks you to send money (Hinglish)**: Unexpected money request.
- `apk_file` [apk, weight 0.75] **Pushes an APK file**: Installing an APK sent by message can give attackers control of your phone and OTPs.
- `remote_access` [apk, weight 0.7] **Asks you to install a remote-access app**: Remote-access apps (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, QuickSupport) let a stranger see your screen and OTPs. Refund and support "agents" use them to empty bank accounts.
- `app_install` [apk, weight 0.2] **Asks you to install an app**: Only install apps from the official Play Store / App Store listing you found yourself.
- `callback_number` [callback, weight 0.22] **Asks you to call or message a mobile number**: Scam messages often give a number that connects you to the fraudster, not the real organisation.
- `prize` [prize, weight 0.45] **Prize, lottery or reward lure**: You cannot win a contest you never entered.
- `prize_hinglish` [prize, weight 0.45] **Prize or lottery lure (Hinglish/Hindi)**: You cannot win a contest you never entered.
- `job_task` [job, weight 0.5] **Easy-money job or task offer**: Pay-per-like and "simple task" jobs are the entry point of task and investment scams.
- `investment` [investment, weight 0.5] **Guaranteed-return investment pitch**: No legitimate investment guarantees returns.
- `delivery` [delivery, weight 0.3] **Parcel / delivery problem lure**: Fake "your parcel is held" messages lead to fee or data-theft pages.
- `refund_lure` [refund, weight 0.4] **Refund or "sent by mistake" lure**: Wrong-transfer and refund stories are used to extract money or credentials.
- `family_emergency` [family, weight 0.45] **Family-emergency money request**: "Hi Mum, new number" and emergency stories impersonate relatives. Call them on their known number.
- `greeting` [greeting, weight 0.12] **Generic greeting**: Real banks usually address you by name.
- `replacement_group` [replacement, weight 0.5] **"Our old group was banned, join the new one"**: SEBI warns that scammers claim a group was banned to move you into a new one they control.
- `stock_club` [investment, weight 0.5] **"VIP" or "institutional" trading group pitch**: SEBI flags invitation-only WhatsApp groups with fake profit claims as a common route to investment fraud.
- `fake_trading_app` [investment, weight 0.5] **Pushes a trading app or "IPO allotment" deposit**: Fake trading apps show fake profits until you try to withdraw. Do not open accounts on apps pushed by strangers.
- `exe_file` [apk, weight 0.7] **Sends a Windows program file**: I4C warned of fake "Statement of Account" and regulator files that are really programs which take over WhatsApp and banking sessions.
- `extortion` [extortion, weight 0.6] **Threatens to leak photos or contact your contacts**: Illegal loan apps and blackmailers threaten to share morphed photos. Paying does not stop it.
- `loan_lure` [loan, weight 0.4] **Instant-loan lure or overdue-loan pressure**: Fake loan apps mimic real lenders to steal data and permissions. Check the RBI directory of digital lending apps first.
- `device_link` [takeover, weight 0.5] **"Vote for my friend" or pairing-code trick**: These tricks make you complete WhatsApp's own device-linking step, silently adding the attacker's browser to your account.
- `task_prepay` [job, weight 0.7] **Task job now asks you to pay in advance**: Task scams pay small amounts first, then demand deposits to "unlock" more. You never get the deposit back.
- `overpayment` [refund, weight 0.5] **"I overpaid, please send the extra back"**: Overpayment stories, often with a fake payment screenshot, trick you into sending real money. Check your own bank balance, not their screenshot.
- `screen_share` [apk, weight 0.3] **Asks you to share your screen**: Screen sharing shows scammers your OTPs and banking apps in real time.

## 6. Advice lines by category

- `credential`: Never share an OTP, PIN, CVV or password with anyone, however official they sound.
- `payment`: Do not pay any "fee", fine or deposit to receive a prize, parcel, refund or loan.
- `authority`: Police, courts and agencies do not arrest or investigate people over calls or chats. Hang up and verify through the official number you look up yourself.
- `isolation`: Anyone telling you to keep a matter secret is manipulating you. Tell a family member or friend now.
- `apk`: Do not install apps sent in messages. Use only the official Play Store or App Store listing.
- `prize`: You cannot win a contest you did not enter. Ignore the message.
- `job`: Real employers do not pay per like or task, and never ask you to deposit money first.
- `investment`: No investment is guaranteed. Do not join "VIP" groups or transfer money to personal accounts.
- `delivery`: Track parcels only in the courier or shop app you already use.
- `kyc`: Do KYC only in your bank's own app or branch, never through a link in a message.
- `utility`: Check bills in your electricity provider's official app. Real disconnections are not announced by random texts.
- `refund`: If money arrives by mistake, ask your bank to reverse it. Do not send it back yourself.
- `family`: Call the relative on their saved number before sending anything.
- `link_impersonation`: Do not open the link. Type the organisation's address yourself or use its official app.
- `link_obfuscation`: Do not open links whose destination is hidden or disguised.
- `link_apk`: Do not download or install the file.
- `callback`: Do not call numbers given in suspicious messages. Use the number printed on your card or official website.
- `replacement`: A "banned group, join the new one" message is a trap. Do not join. Real advisers never need you to switch groups.
- `extortion`: Do not pay blackmailers; it does not stop them. Screenshot the threats, tell your family first, and report at cybercrime.gov.in or on 1930.
- `loan`: Borrow only through lenders listed in the RBI directory of digital lending apps, and never allow a loan app to read your contacts or photos.
- `takeover`: Never type a WhatsApp code into a website or give it to anyone. Check Settings > Linked devices and log out any device you do not recognise.
- `urgency`: Slow down. Pressure to act fast is the scammer's main tool.
- `threat`: Threats of blocking or arrest are designed to scare you. Contact the organisation through its official app.

### You replied to the sender (`replied`)
1. Stop replying. Block the number and report it in WhatsApp or your SMS app.
2. Be alert for follow-up calls: the sender now knows your number is active.
3. If you shared any personal detail, treat it as exposed and watch your accounts.
4. Report the message at sancharsaathi.gov.in ("Report Suspected Fraud Communication", Chakshu).

### You shared an OTP, PIN, CVV or password (`shared`)
1. Act within minutes: call your bank (number on your card or official app) and ask them to block the card, UPI or net banking.
2. Change the password or PIN you shared from a trusted device.
3. If money or account access is lost, call 1930 and file at cybercrime.gov.in as soon as possible.
4. Report the message at sancharsaathi.gov.in ("Report Suspected Fraud Communication", Chakshu).

### You installed an app from the message (`installed`)
1. Turn on airplane mode / switch off mobile data and Wi-Fi to cut the app's connection.
2. Uninstall the app (Settings > Apps). If it will not uninstall, boot to safe mode and remove it.
3. From a different device, change your banking, email and UPI passwords and call your bank.
4. If money or account access is lost, call 1930 and file at cybercrime.gov.in as soon as possible.
5. Report the message at sancharsaathi.gov.in ("Report Suspected Fraud Communication", Chakshu).

### You sent money (`paid`)
1. Call 1930 immediately and file at cybercrime.gov.in. Speed matters for any chance of intervention; no result can be promised.
2. Call your bank right away, give the transaction details and ask them to flag the transfer.
3. Keep evidence: screenshots of the chat, number, transaction ID, UPI ID or account used. Do not delete the conversation.
4. Do not pay again to "recover" the money. That is a second scam.
5. Report the message at sancharsaathi.gov.in ("Report Suspected Fraud Communication", Chakshu).
